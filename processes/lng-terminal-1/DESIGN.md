`scripts/terminal_sizing.py` (run with `cheme run scripts/terminal_sizing.py`); this file keeps

## 2. Decisions

**D-001 Scope and battery limits.** The terminal of an export plant: from the LNG rundown of
the trains (battery limit at the tank area) to the carrier's manifold, with the tank and
vapour-return boil-off compressed back to the trains. Outside: the liquefaction trains (they
are the source of the rundown and the sink of the BOG), the flare derrick steel and the
other three flares on it, the liquid burner, firewater, N2 and instrument-air generation,
power. Rejected: an import terminal (Hazira, Shell's only operated import terminal; the user
chose an export terminal) and a liquefaction scope (four trains would dwarf the terminal).

**D-002 Reference project.** LNG Canada (Shell 40 %, operator LNG Canada Development Inc.,
EPC JGC-Fluor), the newest Shell-led LNG project with public as-built marine data (S-01) and a
Phase 2 decision (S-07). The terminal is a design case *modelled on* it, not a description
of it: where the project has not published a value, ours is an assumption and says so.

**D-003 Capacity.** 28 MTPA from 4 trains (Phase 1 + 2, S-07), 95 % availability (ASSUMED):
3,365 t/h of rundown, ~7,560 m3/h. Phase 1 alone (14 MTPA, 1 tank, 1 berth) is the as-built
subset: T-2101, Berth 2, K-7101A/B.

**D-004 LNG composition.** No LNG specification is public. The LNG is derived from the feed
gas of S-06: CO2, He and C5+ removed in the trains, N2 reduced to 0.3 mol% by the end flash
(ASSUMED), the hydrocarbons in their feed ratio. Result (mol): CH4 0.9335, C2H6 0.0449, C3H8
0.0136, iC4 0.0021, nC4 0.0029, N2 0.0030; molar mass 17.30 kg/kmol.

**D-005 Rundown.** −161.5 degC, 215 kPa(a) at the tank inlet (ASSUMED: subcooled ~1.3 K below
the tank bubble point so the rundown does not flash in the fill line). The subcooling
condenses part of the tank vapour; it is not credited (conservative for BOG).

**D-006 Property method outside cheme.** `cheme process props --coverage` finds no
benchmarked package for LNG or methane-rich vapour at −160 degC (the packages cover air and
syngas). The script uses CoolProp's HEOS mixture model with the GERG-2008 departure functions,
the reference equation of state for natural gas: bubble point −160.2 degC at 115 kPa(a), LNG
density 445 kg/m3, incipient boil-off 92.1 % CH4 / 7.9 % N2 (mol). Latent heat of the boil-off
taken as that of methane (508 kJ/kg). Stream values are therefore `assumed` with the script as
their source (library ADR 0027); `calculated` values would need a cheme package.

**D-007 Tanks.** 2 full-containment tanks, 225,000 m3 working each (tank 1: S-08; tank 2:
sources give 225,000 or 255,000 m3, 225,000 ASSUMED). 92 m outer diameter, 56 m to the dome
top (S-08); inner tank ~88 m x ~41 m liquid height (ASSUMED). Operating pressure 115 kPa(a),
design +29 / −0.5 kPa(g), boil-off 0.05 %/d of a full tank (all ASSUMED, typical of the type).
Pressure control to the BOG compressors, then to the storage and loading flare, then the
tank's PSVs to atmosphere; vacuum valves and a make-up gas line against vacuum (D-017).

**D-008 Loading lines and circulation.** 2 loading lines DN 800 and 2 vapour return lines DN
750 over the trestle (S-04: up to two of each). At 12,000 m3/h (S-05) each loading line
carries 6,000 m3/h at 3.4 m/s and loses ~91 kPa over 2.45 km. In holding one circulation pump
(S-05) sends LNG out on one loading line and back on the other through a crossover at the
platform, ~314 m3/h for a 3 K rise against ~380 kW heat ingress; the pump is rated 500 m3/h
(ASSUMED). Rejected: a dedicated small return line (two DN 800 lines already form the loop).

**D-009 Operating cases and the BOG balance.** Three cases (`process/flowsheet.yaml`):
*holding* (no carrier: the rundown fills the tanks, its displaced vapour 14.9 t/h, the tank
boil-off 4.2 t/h and the circulation heat 2.9 t/h make 21.9 t/h of BOG); *loading* one carrier
at 12,000 m3/h while the rundown continues (the carrier returns 45.6 t/h: its displaced
vapour at −120 degC, its cargo-tank heat-in of 1.5 MW and 85 % of the pump and line heat
flashing on board, all ASSUMED; the tanks' growing vapour space absorbs the tank boil-off);
*average* the year, loading 62.8 % of the time so that the tank inventory is steady (the
default and LCA case). The tank inventory change is a boundary stream (S11, S12). The tank
liquid weathers: the N2-rich boil-off leaves it slightly heavier than the rundown, and the
balance carries that composition. No shore vapour blower: the carrier's own return-gas
compressor drives the return (S-01).

**D-010 BOG compression.** 3 x 50 % centrifugal compressors (one spare), each 27 t/h (50 %
of the loading case + 10 %), suction 108 kPa(a) at −140 degC, discharge 600 kPa(a) to the
trains' end-flash / fuel gas system (ASSUMED), ~1.4 MW each (ideal gas, 78 % polytropic). An
LNG spray desuperheater (3.5 t/h at loading) cools the −120 degC return vapour to −140 degC
upstream of the suction drum D-7101. Holding needs one machine. Rejected: reciprocating
machines (flow too large), a shore return blower (S-01).

**D-011 In-tank pumps and hydraulics.** Per tank 4 submerged-motor loading pumps of 3,000
m3/h (ASSUMED) and 1 circulation pump of 500 m3/h: any 4 of the 8 loading pumps make 12,000
m3/h, so either tank alone can load a carrier. Differential 577 kPa (132 m): manifold
pressure 350 kPa(a) (ASSUMED), 30 m static to the manifold, 91 kPa line loss, 120 kPa for
arms, ERS, strainers and valves (ASSUMED). ~640 kW shaft each.

**D-012 Insulation and heat ingress.** PIR/PUF 200 mm on the DN 800 loading lines, 150 mm on
the DN 750 vapour lines, k = 0.025 W/m K, +20 % for supports and valves, 5 degC mean ambient
(ASSUMED): ~380 kW on the two loading lines, ~380 kW on the two vapour lines.

**D-013 Site geometry.** The plot plan follows the LNG Canada site: tank 1 centre, the flare
derrick and the train blocks from OSM (S-11), the trestle start and the Berth 2 loading
platform from imagery (S-12, ±15–20 m), converted to a local frame by
`scripts/site_frame.py` (origin 53.990 N, 128.700 W, x east, y north). Measured: tank 1 to the
flare 561 m at 82 deg; tank 1 to the Berth 2 platform 2,147 m at 174 deg; trestle 1,982 m at
178 deg from the plant's south edge; berth face 375 m at 173.5 deg; train pitch 210 m. One
datum: z = 0 is grade everywhere (the real tank area is ~12 m above the jetty deck datum;
ignored at concept). Line lengths: 2.45 km per trestle line (trestle plus tank area and
platform runs). OSM is used for a few rounded points only (insubstantial extract,
attribution here and in `scripts/site_frame.py`).

**D-014 Berths and arms.** Two berths on a T-head loading platform: Berth 2 (as built, S-01)
on the west face, Berth 1 (Phase 2) on the east face (ASSUMED, D-019). Each berth has 4 x 16
inch arms south to north liquid, vapour, hybrid, liquid (S-01); the hybrid arm normally runs
as the third liquid arm (3 x ~4,000 m3/h = 12,000 m3/h) and is the spare vapour arm (line-ups
in D-026). Each arm
has an ERS (two ball valves and a powered emergency release coupler) and a quick
connect/disconnect coupler (EN 1474 practice); the arm envelope (22 m, 45 t) is ASSUMED.
Design carrier 217,000 m3 (terminal maximum, S-01); the certified envelope is 345 m (S-04).

**D-015 Tags and sheets.** Tags `<letters>-<sheet digit><nnn>[A-D]` (equipment), ISA 5.1
letters + `<sheet digit><nnn>` (instruments), `<fluid>-<sheet digit><nnn>` (lines; fluid codes
LNG, BOG, FL flare, DR drain, N nitrogen, IA instrument air, FG fuel gas). Sheets by system:
P-01 rundown, P-02 and P-03 the two tanks with their pumps, P-04 the trestle lines, P-05 and
P-06 the two berths, P-07 BOG, P-08 flare, impoundment and utilities. P-03 and P-06 are
copies of P-02 and P-05 renumbered (identical trains of equipment).

**D-016 ESD and ERS philosophy.** ESD-1 stops the loading pumps and closes the shore ESD
valves at the platform with a closure time chosen against surge (ASSUMED 25–30 s, checked in
detail engineering) and trips the carrier through the ship-shore link (S-01, Trelleborg
fibre-optic); ESD-2 also opens the ERS (dry break) when an arm reaches its envelope limit or
on the 35 kn disconnect (S-01). Surge relief from the platform header to the jetty drain drum
D-5101 / D-6101 (ASSUMED). Cause and effect in `control/cause-effect.yaml`.

**D-017 Relief and flare philosophy.** Tank overpressure: pressure control to the BOG
compressors, then PV to the storage and loading flare FL-8101 via the KO drum D-8101 (S-02:
a storage and loading flare exists), then tank PSVs to atmosphere (last resort, sized for
fire and loss of the BOG system). Tank vacuum: make-up from the trains' fuel gas (FG-1001,
FO-1117, PV-1111; D-025), then vacuum valves. Thermal relief valves on every blocked-in cryogenic section, discharging to the
vapour system. Carrier de-inerting and cool-down vapour to the storage and loading flare
(S-02).

**D-018 Spacing and spill containment.** Tank to tank shell to shell ≥ a quarter of the sum of
the diameters (NFPA 59A practice, measured 48 m); tank to the BOG compressors ≥ 30 m; the
loading point ≥ 200 m from ignition sources and the public (S-05) and the 200 m vapour-arm
safety zone (S-01); flare radiation ≤ 5.0 kW/m2 at the fence (S-10). Spills: troughs along the
tank-area rack and drip trays on the trestle drain to the impoundment sump T-8101 (ASSUMED
10 min of one loading line); the platform has its own sump T-8102. Distances quoted are
`separations:` of `layout/site-plan.yaml`, measured by `cheme validate`.

**D-019 Phase 2 representation.** Not public: where the second tank and the second berth
stand. ASSUMED: T-3101 140 m west of T-2101 (centre to centre, towards trains 3 and 4), Berth 1
on the east face of the same loading platform (T-head), both identical to their Phase 1
counterparts. The Phase 1 subset can be read off the sheets: P-02, P-05 and K-7101A/B.

**D-020 Utilities.** Electric drives throughout (grid supply, S-05); 6.6 kV for the in-tank
pumps and the BOG compressors, 400 V otherwise (ASSUMED). N2 for arm purge and leak test,
seal gas and blanketing, and instrument air, from the site systems at the battery limit. Drawn
at basic: the berth N2 tie-ins N-8001 / N-8002 (P-05, P-06, P-08) and the pump-column N2 hose
connections (P-02, P-03); not drawn at basic (D-026.9): the compressor seal gas and purge
(vendor package), the tank inerting points (tank contractor) and the trestle purge points.

**D-021 P&ID drafting conventions (basic stage).** The eight sheets (A3, DEXPI 2.0.1) are
generated sheets: each is built by its script on cheme-as-code's authoring API
(`scripts/draft_P-01.py` ... `draft_P-08.py`; P-02/P-03 run `draft_tank.py` and P-05/P-06
`draft_berth.py` with the sheet digit; helpers in `scripts/lng_pid.py`) on its empty frame in
`pid/frames/`, declared with `generator:` in `plant.yaml` and rebuilt with `cheme regen`; the
scripts are the source, the XML is never edited by hand. Each draft recorded the lines, instruments and
relief devices it drew, and `scripts/build_lists.py` wrote the line list, instrument list and
special items from them, so the lists and the sheets started from the same values.
Conventions:
1. Two symbols the catalogue lacks are our own drawings: the full-containment tank
   (concrete outer tank with dome, dashed 9 % Ni inner tank, suspended deck) and the marine
   loading arm (riser, inboard and outboard arm, counterweight, swivel).
2. Relief valves are drawn as globe valves and turned into `SpringLoadedGlobeSafetyValve` (the
   vacuum relief valves `BreatherValve`) with the spring-loaded symbol, as ammonia-1 does.
3. All nozzles of a full-containment tank pass through the roof; the sheets draw the bottom
   fill, the circulation return, the relief valves and the instrument connections on the shell
   for legibility.
4. A line keeps its number across sheets (e.g. BOG-1001 on P-02, P-01 and P-07). Where the
   pipe size changes at a sheet boundary the number changes with it: pump header LNG-2001 /
   LNG-3001 -> trestle LNG-4001 / LNG-4002 -> berth header LNG-5001 / LNG-6001, and berth
   vapour BOG-5001 / BOG-6001 -> trestle BOG-4001 / BOG-4002; the off-page note names the line
   on the other sheet and the finding is accepted with this reason (`checks/checks.yaml`).
5. A header that starts at its first pump (LNG-2001, LNG-3001) or compressor (BOG-7002) has
   that machine's column or discharge as segment S1 at the smaller size, listed as such.
6. The berth sheets draw the arms left to right L, L, H, V (the platform order south to north
   is L, V, H, L, S-01) so that the vapour line leaves the sheet without crossings.
7. Not drawn at basic: expansion loops, supports and drip trays of the trestle, low-point
   drains and vents of the long lines, instrument root valves, the fire and gas system.
8. Each ERS is two ball valves with their own UZ functions either side of the coupler; the
   coupler and the quick connect/disconnect coupler are part of the arm (vendor package).
9. A pump started and stopped by a controller is drawn with a motor control function HS at the
   pump, tied to it by a measuring line (run feedback), and a signal line from the controller
   to the HS (the authoring API signals only valves and instrument functions); a duty/standby
   pair is driven as a chain (LIC-8101 -> HS-8101A -> HS-8101B on P-08, D-026.11).
Since review 2026-11-05-basic-design-3 (#10) the frames are committed in `pid/frames/` and
`cheme validate` reports a hand edit that the script would lose as `generated-sheet-drift`.

**D-022 Datasheets (detail stage).** `cheme datasheets draft` wrote one datasheet per item;
`cheme datasheets fill` cannot size them (no property package for LNG, D-006, and most items
have no flowsheet stream of their own), so `scripts/datasheet_values.py` completed them once
from `scripts/terminal_sizing.py` and D-007 to D-018, every value with its source and ASSUMED
where it is: in-tank loading pumps 3,000 m3/h at 132 m, ~640 kW shaft (efficiency 0.75); the
circulation pumps 500 m3/h at 60 m; the BOG compressors 27 t/h, 108 -> 600 kPa(a), 1,397 kW gas
power, 1,426 kW shaft. Curves, seals, motors and the arm envelopes are `supplier`.

**D-023 Surge and vapour arm capacity (closes the two open points of the control
philosophy).** (1) Surge: the loading line period 2L/a is 4.3 s (a ~1,150 m/s, ASSUMED); an ESD
closure in 25 s (ASSUMED, ESD-1 stops the pumps first) is slow, and the slow-closure estimate
2 rho L v / t gives +299 kPa, a peak of ~990 kPa(a) (8.9 barg) at the shore ESD valve, inside
the 15 barg design of the lines and the arms (instant closure would add 1,753 kPa: the closure
time is a safety requirement, recorded in I-501). A transient analysis in the supplier's tool
confirms it before construction. (2) Vapour arm: one 16 inch arm carries ~23.5 t/h of the
-120 degC return at 40 m/s (ASSUMED limit); the balance's 45.6 t/h (D-009) is the conservative
design case of the BOG system (85 % of the pump and line heat flashing on board). Above the
arm's capacity the carrier keeps its tank pressure by burning gas on board (allowed during
loading, S-01) or the loading rate is reduced; the hybrid arm is the spare vapour path (the two
arm line-ups of D-026). Both checked against carrier data in detail engineering.

**D-024 LCA scope.** Gate-to-gate inventory per tonne of LNG loaded, in the annual-average
case (`lca/lca.yaml`): 0.993 t of rundown and 1.13 kWh of electricity per tonne loaded; the
returned BOG is a co-product allocated by lower heating value (LNG 49.3, BOG 43.6 MJ/kg). The
carrier's return vapour is part of the cargo and carries no burden; the inventory change is
zero over the year. The rundown and the BC grid electricity are gaps until a liquefaction and
a BC Hydro background are linked; fugitive methane, the flare pilots and purge and the
construction phase are not in the foreground yet (a screening carbon footprint, `lca/results.md`).

verifier, changed the P&IDs, the C&E and the sequences:
1. Rundown (P-01): thermal relief on both fill branches (PSV-1122/1123); position switches
   ZSC-1102/1103 and I-102 close the battery limit and trip the trains when no tank can take
   the rundown; the high-high tank pressure trip acts on its own SIS vent XV-1115 in a bypass of
   PV-1110 (fail closed, energise-to-trip with its own air accumulator: a fail-open vent with the
   fail-closed make-up would depressurise the tanks on a utility failure); the make-up gas comes
   from the trains' fuel gas (FG-1001, independent of the terminal's compressors) through a
   restriction orifice FO-1117 and PV-1111, which is regulatory only (the vacuum trip stops the
   withdrawal and the compressors; the vacuum valves are the last protection).
2. Tanks (P-02/P-03): N+1 pressure PSVs and vacuum valves (A/B/C), vacuum set -0.3 kPa(g); a
   second independent level LZT-n106, I-n01 now 1oo2 and also closing the circulation return;
   thermal relief of the pump and circulation headers (PSV-n141/n142); an isolation valve and a

3. Trestle (P-04): thermal relief of the crossover and the circulation return (PSV-4113/4114);

**D-026 Response to the follow-up review 2026-11-05-basic-design-2.**
1. Vapour return switch (#1). The de-inerting switch is make-before-break in both directions
   and proven by open-position switches ZSO-4131/4132/4135/4136 (P-04): I-511 opens the flare
   valve on off-specification vapour, I-512 closes the tank-side valve only with the flare
   valve proven open, I-513 closes the flare valve only with the tank-side valve proven open.
   ESD-1 (I-501 / I-601) opens the tank-side valve and does not act on the flare valve, so an
   ESD-1, including the test of SEQ-LOAD S3, never closes the vapour return, the relief path of
   the berth. The tank-side valves XV-4135/4136 fail open and the flare valves XV-4131/4132
   fail closed (review 2026-11-05-basic-design-3#1): on an instrument-air failure the return
   goes to the tanks and the tank vapour header is never opened to the flare through the
   switch (the reason XV-1115 fails closed, D-025). I-511 does not open the flare valve while
   ESD-1 is tripped, so I-511 and I-513 never command it against each other (review 3 #2).
2. ESD-2 (previous #4). I-501 / I-601 take I-503 / I-603 tripped as a cause, so ESD-2 trips
   ESD-1 at once (pumps, shore ESD valve, tank-end valves, the carrier's ESD). The 5 s delay is
