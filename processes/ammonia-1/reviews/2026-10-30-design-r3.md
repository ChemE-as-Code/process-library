## The fixes, checked

- **Z-HAZ-FUEL (layout#4).** `cheme show H-101` confirms the mapping: nozzle B2 takes FG-1002
  (fuel natural gas) and B1 takes FG-1010 (PGR tail gas). The source substances match. Hydrogen
  for the tail gas is the conservative choice, because the tail gas is mostly H2, CH4, N2 and Ar.
  The grade is secondary and the zone is 2, both ASSUMED with D-031. Putting the release source
  at the nozzle stands in for valve trains that have not been placed yet. That is stated, so it
  is honest. Whether the radius covers the real train locations is a question for the layout
  review and the package layout. Nothing else in the design changed.
- **PC-1048/PC-1084 pins.** The routing.yaml diff removes the 6 cm dip of PC-1048 (the point at
  9.01 m now runs at 9.07 m). It also replaces the 9.5 m rise of PC-1084 with a fall
  8.0 -> 6.5 -> 5.5 m to the nozzle. Both are now free-draining towards U-801, which is what
  D-031 says. The MTO change (-4 m DN25, -6 elbows) agrees with that.
- **design#1 response.** PROGRESS.md splits the 26 blocked lanes into 9 erection-sequence cases
  and 17 own-piping cases. It explains why only a lifting plan and a human can accept them. It
  also says why the class 4 estimate needs datasheet sizes (S7), a sourced CEPCI and sourced bulk
  rates, and why maturity-balanced needs S1/S2 sourcing. The account is accurate. Nothing was
  accepted, relaxed or invented. But the finding is still not closed, and runs/interventions.md
  has no entry after O-029 that moves the estimate, the lifting acceptance or the maturity gate
  out of S5r.

### Spot-checks of cited values

- `scripts/estimate_weights.py:12,31`: the carbon-steel allowable stress S = 138 MPa. This is
  ASME VIII-1's 20.0 ksi (137.9 MPa) for SA-516 Gr 70 at moderate temperature. **Confirmed.**
- `process/flowsheet.yaml:423`: the natural gas feed plus fuel is 37,714.5 kg/h for 1500 t/d NH3
  (62.5 t/h; D-006, ASSUMED). With the D-004 lean gas (91 % CH4, 4 % C2H6, 1 % C3H8, 1 % CO2,
  3 % N2), the LHV is about 46 MJ/kg. That gives about 27.8 GJ(LHV)/t NH3 of gas, plus 36.6 MW of
  electricity (about 2.1 GJ/t) for an all-electric plant. From general engineering knowledge,
  modern steam-reforming plants use roughly 28-33 GJ/t, so this is at the efficient end but
  plausible. The BREF itself was not opened in this review. **Consistent.** The provenance is
  honestly `assumed`, and that is the cause of the sourced-boundary failure.
