### Spot-checks of cited values

- **Sharma et al. (2024), doi:10.1299/jtst.24-00063** (process/reactions.yaml NH3_COMB,
  D-034). I fetched the J-STAGE article page. The title, authors and J. Therm. Sci. Technol.
  19(2) all match. The fuel is 30 % NH3 / 70 % CH4 on an LHV basis, the air is preheated to
  about 1000 C, and NH3 and N2O were hardly detected at the exhaust. **Confirmed.** The
  "40 kW" in D-034 is not on the article page (finding r3#3).
- **K-102 / K-103 fan sizes and costs** (estimate/cost-basis.yaml, equipment-cost.csv).
  - Air at 288.15 K and 1 atm has a density of 101325 x 28.965 / (8314.46 x 288.15) =
    1.225 kg/m3. So 165407 kg/h gives 37.5 m3/s.
  - The flue gas density is 100000 x 27.92 / (8314.46 x 423.15) = 0.794 kg/m3. So
    176411 kg/h gives 61.8 m3/s.
  - The Turton radial fan correlation (K 3.5391, -0.3533, 0.4477) gives Cp0 = 12.37 k USD
    and 22.0 k USD (2001), which match 12,365 and 21,999.
  - **Confirmed.**
- **Turton et al. (2012) section 7.3**: the total module cost is 1.18 x the bare module
  cost, made up of contingency 0.15 and fee 0.03. This agrees with the reviewer's knowledge
  of the text (not re-downloaded). Towler and Sinnott Table 6.3 lists D&E 0.30 for fluids and
  a contingency of 0.10, which also agrees.
